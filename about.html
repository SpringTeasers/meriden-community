About Meriden

A New Haven County city halfway between New Haven and Hartford — known as The Silver City, settled in 1661 and a town since 1806.

The Silver City

Meriden's identity was built on manufacturing. The city is known as The Silver City, a nickname that comes from its once-thriving silver industry. Alongside silver, Meriden's industrial history includes cutlery and gun manufacturing.

The city's civic story starts early. Meriden was settled in 1661 and was officially recognised as a town in 1806. It became a city in 1867.

One development shaped the city's growth decisively: the arrival of the railroad in 1839 shifted the centre of the town, and that shift fostered the growth and development that followed.

A short timeline

  1. 1661 Meriden is settled.
  2. 1806 Officially recognised as a town.
  3. 1839 The railroad arrives, shifting the centre of the town and driving growth.
  4. 1867 Meriden becomes a city.

A city of about 60,850

Meriden's population is approximately 60,850 as of the 2020 census. The city covers 24.16 square miles (62.58 km²) and sits at an elevation of 177 ft (54 m).

How the city is run

Meriden operates under a City Manager–City Council form of government. The city is led by a Mayor and an elected council, with various appointed boards and commissions overseeing different functions within the city.

Mayor and City Council

An elected Mayor and council set policy for the city, under the City Manager–City Council form of government.
